I am a postdoctoral researcher in developmental psychology, and my research is driven by my curiosity to understand how babies come to understand objects, actions, and the words their parents use to describe them. I examine early cognitive development in infancy, focusing on _when_ and _how_ babies learn words and the role that access to language plays in how they learn to recognise objects in the world around them. 

I conducted my doctoral studies at the [Cardiff University Centre for Human Developmental Science](https://www.cardiff.ac.uk/research/explore/research-units/centre-for-human-developmental-science), exploring early verb learning, and I received my PhD in 2023. Learning verbs is considerably harder than learning nouns because they describe fleeting actions rather than concrete objects. _When_ and _how_ infants learn verbs is an open question. My PhD research demonstrated that infants begin overcoming the verb learning challenge earlier than expected, understanding their first verbs as young as 10 months old. I also found that early verb learning is strongly tied to infants’ motor development, suggesting that infants’ own motor and action experience may play a role in their verb learning.

Since 2023, I have been a postdoctoral researcher at the University of East Anglia investigating object categorisation. In this role, I have been using infant EEG to find new ways to capture what object categories – such as books, balls, and cows – babies recognise from their experiences at home. Specifically, I use machine learning approaches to EEG – known as Multivariate Pattern Analysis – to decode category familiarity from infants’ brain activity. I now use these techniques with deaf babies born to hearing families, to understand whether access to language helps babies learn about these categories in their day-to-day life.

Across my work, I use a range of techniques to explore early development including EEG, eye-tracking, caregiver-infant interaction, standardised measures of development (such as the Mullen Scales of Early Learning and Preschool Language Scale), and caregiver report measures.
